## Case Study: Stabilizing a Failing Production System (TransUnion)

I was brought into an environment where production issues were happening regularly and the cost of instability was already showing up in the business.

This wasn’t a greenfield problem. The system was live, under load, and failing in ways that weren’t fully understood.

### Situation

* Production incidents averaging ~3 per week
* Revenue impact tied directly to system instability
* Multiple interacting components across application, infrastructure, and database layers
* No single, clear root cause

### What I Did

I started by treating the system as a whole instead of isolating individual components.

* **Used proprietary assessment software I developed to analyze the full system (application, infrastructure, and data layers), producing a detailed 70-page report covering architecture, failure modes, and remediation paths**

* Reviewed application behavior, infrastructure, and database design together

* Identified failure patterns rather than chasing individual incidents

* Guided technologists in remediation of incorrect configuration

* Traced how data flow and system interactions were breaking under real conditions

* Prioritized fixes based on impact, not just visibility

### What Changed

* Reduced incidents from ~3 per week to 0 within one month
* Stabilized the system enough for the business to operate without constant interruption
* Avoided an estimated ~$6M in annual revenue loss

### Output

* Delivered a detailed remediation roadmap (~70 pages)
* Provided a clear path from reactive firefighting to controlled system behavior

### Why This Matters

A lot of teams try to fix instability one incident at a time.

That approach doesn’t scale.

You have to:

* understand the system as a set of interactions
* identify the patterns behind failures
* and apply changes in a way that shifts the system’s behavior, not just individual symptoms

Once that happens, stability stops being temporary and becomes part of how the system operates.
